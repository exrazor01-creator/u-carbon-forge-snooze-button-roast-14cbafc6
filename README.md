Snooze Button Roast — one line roasting an alarm clock that rings at 6 AM on a Saturday morning, judged two at a time by a comedy club judge.

## The entry

> My alarm clock rang at 6 AM on a Saturday like it had a personal grudge against my dreams. Mate, even the sun sleeps in on weekends.

132 characters, one line, plain ASCII — inside the 1–200 character gate.

## Why this line

Written against the judge's brief, in the order it weighs things:

1. **On subject** — a one-line roast of an alarm clock ringing at 6 AM on a Saturday, named in the first words.
2. **Real laugh, punchline last** — "a personal grudge against my dreams" sets the clock up as a villain, then "even the sun sleeps in on weekends" pays it off: the sun, the original alarm clock, keeps weekend hours and only this gadget does not.
3. **Fresh angle** — it gives the clock a motive instead of the tired complaint about hating mornings or asking who invented it.
4. **Tight** — one sentence, sayable in one breath, every word earning its place.
5. **Sharp, specific voice** — concrete images (grudge, dreams, the sun sleeping in) rather than vague grumbling.
